# Blog Content Guidelines - MedSpa Flywheel

## ⚠️ CRITICAL: No Fabricated Case Studies or Statistics

**NEVER create fake client examples, made-up statistics, or fabricated case studies.**

This includes:
- ❌ "An Atlanta med spa we worked with saw 180% growth..."
- ❌ "Our client in Nashville increased bookings by 45%..."
- ❌ "A Charlotte med spa implemented our system and saved $10,000..."
- ❌ Any specific numbers attributed to unnamed clients
- ❌ Any "real example" that isn't actually real

**Why this matters:**
- Damages credibility if discovered
- Could be considered false advertising
- Violates trust with readers
- Potential legal issues with FTC guidelines

---

## ✅ What You CAN Use

### 1. Industry-Standard Benchmarks (WITH SOURCES)

**Good Examples:**

```
According to WordStream's 2024 benchmarks, healthcare Google Ads campaigns average:
- CTR: 3.27%
- CPC: $2.62
- Conversion rate: 3.36%
```

```
The DMA reports that email marketing delivers an average ROI of $42 per $1 spent.
```

```
HubSpot's 2024 Marketing Report shows that companies with CRM systems see
29% increase in sales and 34% improvement in customer satisfaction.
```

**Required elements:**
- ✅ Attribution to source (WordStream, DMA, HubSpot, etc.)
- ✅ Year of data
- ✅ Industry-specific when possible
- ✅ Conservative, realistic numbers

### 2. Clearly Labeled Hypothetical Examples

**Good Example:**

```
Example Scenario: If a med spa spends $2,000/month on Google Ads with
industry-average performance (3.27% CTR, 15% conversion rate), they could
expect approximately 20-30 qualified leads per month.
```

**Required:**
- ✅ Must use "Example Scenario", "Hypothetical Example", or "Sample Calculation"
- ✅ Must show the math/assumptions
- ✅ Use conservative estimates
- ✅ Never attribute to a real or implied client

### 3. General Range Statements

**Good Examples:**

```
"Med spas typically see a 4-6x ROI on Google Ads campaigns."
"Most practices report 20-30% reduction in no-shows with automated reminders."
"Email open rates for healthcare businesses range from 21-24%."
```

**Required:**
- ✅ Use ranges, not specific numbers
- ✅ Based on industry research
- ✅ Include "typically", "average", "most", etc.

### 4. Your Own Legitimate Results (If Applicable)

**Only if you have:**
- ✅ Written client permission
- ✅ Actual data to back it up
- ✅ Documented proof
- ✅ Client testimonial or case study on file

**Format:**

```
Case Study: [Client Name] Med Spa in [City]

With client permission, we're sharing results from our work with
[specific med spa that gave permission]...

[Include actual, documented results]
```

## 📊 Trusted Sources for Statistics

### Marketing & Advertising
- **WordStream** - Google Ads benchmarks by industry
- **HubSpot** - Annual State of Marketing report
- **Mailchimp** - Email marketing benchmarks
- **Hootsuite** - Social media statistics

### Healthcare & Med Spa Specific
- **American Med Spa Association (AmSpa)** - Industry reports
- **Aesthetic Society** - Annual statistics
- **Medical Spa MD** - Industry research

### General Business
- **Gartner** - CRM and software statistics
- **Forrester** - Customer experience data
- **McKinsey** - Business research

### How to Cite Sources

**In-text citations:**
```html
<p>
    According to WordStream's 2024 benchmarks, healthcare Google Ads
    campaigns average a 3.27% click-through rate.
</p>
```

**Link to source (when available):**
```html
<p>
    According to <a href="https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2024/02/06/google-ads-benchmarks"
    target="_blank" rel="noopener">WordStream's 2024 benchmarks</a>,
    healthcare Google Ads campaigns average a 3.27% click-through rate.
</p>
```

## 🚫 Red Flags to Avoid

### DON'T Write:
- "We helped a med spa in [city] achieve..."
- "One of our clients saw a 180% increase..."
- "Real example: A practice we worked with..."
- Any specific city + specific results combination
- "Our average client sees..." (unless you have documented data)

### DO Write:
- "Industry benchmarks show..."
- "According to [Source], med spas typically see..."
- "Research indicates that practices using [tool] report..."
- "Data from [Source] suggests..."

---

## 📝 Content Creation Checklist

Before publishing ANY blog post, verify:

- [ ] No fabricated case studies or client examples
- [ ] All statistics cited with sources
- [ ] No specific client results without written permission
- [ ] Hypothetical examples clearly labeled as such
- [ ] Conservative, realistic projections (not hype)
- [ ] Industry benchmarks are recent (within 2 years)
- [ ] External sources are reputable and verifiable
- [ ] No guarantees or promises of specific results
- [ ] Compliant with FTC guidelines on testimonials/claims

## 🎯 Example: Rewriting Bad Content

### ❌ BAD (Fabricated):
```
A Nashville med spa added email automation and saw a 25% conversion rate
on welcome series, 35% re-booking rate, and $18,000 additional revenue
in 90 days.
```

### ✅ GOOD (Industry Data):
```
According to the DMA (Data & Marketing Association), email marketing
delivers an average ROI of $42 for every $1 spent. Healthcare industry
emails see average open rates of 21-24% and click-through rates of 2-3%.
Well-crafted welcome series typically convert 15-25% of new subscribers.
```

### ✅ ALSO GOOD (Hypothetical):
```
Example Scenario: A med spa with 500 email subscribers sending 4 campaigns
per month could expect (based on industry average open rates of 22% and
CTR of 2.5%):
- 110 email opens per campaign
- 2-3 clicks per campaign
- Potential for 8-12 monthly bookings from email alone

Note: This is a hypothetical calculation based on industry benchmarks.
```

## 🔍 When in Doubt

**Ask yourself:**
1. Can I cite a reputable source for this claim?
2. Is this data publicly available and verifiable?
3. Would I feel comfortable defending this claim if questioned?
4. Am I being specific enough to be helpful without making things up?

**If you can't answer YES to all four:**
- Remove the specific claim
- Replace with industry benchmark data
- Use a clearly labeled hypothetical example
- Remove the section entirely if no good replacement exists

## 📚 Quick Reference: Acceptable vs. Unacceptable

| ❌ NEVER | ✅ ALWAYS |
|---------|----------|
| "A client of ours in Miami..." | "According to WordStream..." |
| "We helped a med spa achieve..." | "Industry data shows..." |
| Specific city + specific results | General ranges + source |
| Made-up statistics | Cited benchmarks |
| "Real example:" (if not real) | "Example scenario:" or "Hypothetical:" |
| "Our average client sees 180% growth" | "Studies indicate 30-50% improvement is common" |

---

## 🎓 Training for Content Writers

All writers creating blog content must:
1. Read and acknowledge these guidelines
2. Understand FTC guidelines on testimonials and claims
3. Know how to find and cite reputable sources
4. Default to removing content rather than making it up
5. When unsure, ask for review before publishing

---

## 📞 Questions?

When writing blog content, if you're unsure whether something violates these guidelines:
- Remove the questionable content
- Replace with cited industry data
- Flag for review before publishing

**Remember: Credibility takes years to build and seconds to destroy. When in doubt, leave it out.**
